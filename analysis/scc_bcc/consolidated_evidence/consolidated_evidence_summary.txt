DERMASENSE SCC CONSOLIDATED EVIDENCE SUMMARY
================================================================================

DATASET / SAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Matched SCC lesions: 22
Problematic SCC lesions: 11
Clean SCC lesions: 11

PRIMARY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. C1 lesion-level geometry:
   Problematic SCC lesions are substantially more BCC-side
   than clean SCC lesions.
   Raw geometry Mann-Whitney p = 0.025575.

2. Centroid-side analysis:
   C1 problematic SCC: 5/11 closer to BCC centroid.
   C1 clean SCC:       0/11 closer to BCC centroid.
   Therefore 6/11 problematic lesions remain SCC-side,
   while all 11 clean lesions are SCC-side.

3. Clinical size confound:
   Problematic SCC lesions are larger.
   diameter_1: problematic mean 16.27 vs clean 9.55,
   p = 0.012214.
   diameter_2: problematic mean 13.55 vs clean 8.82,
   p = 0.024337.
   lesion-area proxy: problematic mean 241.09 vs clean 115.09,
   p = 0.016418.

4. Size ↔ feature-margin relationship:
   Associations are weak and non-significant.
   C1 diameter_1 Spearman rho = 0.1372, p = 0.5425.
   C1 diameter_2 Spearman rho = 0.1526, p = 0.4977.
   C1 area proxy Spearman rho = 0.1278, p = 0.5710.
   Thus lesion size is strongly associated with error status,
   but size alone does not explain the feature margin.

5. Hurt confound:
   5/5 hurt lesions are problematic and all are large.
   However, among the 17 non-hurt lesions, 6/17 remain
   problematic and the C1 margin difference remains significant:
   p = 0.014544.
   Therefore hurt cannot fully explain the geometry finding.

6. F1 / SupCon displacement:
   Problematic mean margin changed from -0.3392 to -0.4614.
   Clean mean margin changed from -1.5318 to -1.3398.
   Overall paired mean Δ margin = +0.0349.
   Wilcoxon p = 0.222107.
   F1 therefore changed the geometry but did not produce a
   statistically reliable selective correction of problematic SCCs.

INTERPRETATION
-
The evidence is consistent with a lesion-level representation
shift in problematic SCC cases toward the BCC side of the learned
feature space. The effect persists after examining the non-hurt
subgroup and is not explained by a simple linear relationship
between lesion size and feature margin.

However, the sample is small (22 lesions), the problematic/clean
groups are not independent clinical cohorts, and multiple
exploratory tests were performed. The results should therefore
be treated as diagnostic evidence rather than confirmatory proof.

F1 did not clearly solve the problem: some problematic lesions
moved toward the SCC side, but the paired lesion-level analysis
does not show a reliable overall selective improvement.
